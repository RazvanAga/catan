# Catan Web Game — v2: AI bot players

## Problem Statement

My friends and I can now play base-game Catan in our browsers, but only when at
least three humans show up at the same time. Catan needs 3–4 players, so a night
where only two of us are free is a night we can't play. I also sometimes want to
try out an opening or just play a quick game without rounding up a full table.
Today the lobby's "Start game" button stays disabled below three humans and there
is no way to fill the empty chairs — the room simply waits for people who aren't
coming.

## Solution

The room owner can add **bot players** in the lobby to fill empty seats up to the
4-player maximum. Each "Add bot" press seats a computer-controlled player with an
auto-assigned name and an available color, shown in the lobby roster alongside the
humans but clearly marked as a bot. The owner can remove a bot again before
starting. Once at least three seats are filled (humans + bots), the owner starts
the game exactly as before, and from then on the bots take their own turns with no
human input: they place their setup settlements and roads, roll, collect and spend
resources, build, buy and play development cards, move the robber and steal,
discard on a 7, and respond to trades that humans propose. Play never stalls on a
bot — a bot seat always acts promptly and the game runs to a 10-VP victory whether
the winner is human or bot.

The bots play by a simple, legal, pragmatic heuristic. They are not meant to be
strong opponents in v1 of this feature; the goal is a valid, watchable, never-stuck
opponent that lets a short-handed table play a complete game.

## User Stories

### Adding and managing bots in the lobby

1. As the room owner, I want an "Add bot" control in the lobby, so that I can fill an empty seat with a computer player.
2. As the room owner, I want each added bot to receive an available color automatically, so that I don't have to pick one and can't create a color clash.
3. As the room owner, I want each added bot to receive a recognizable auto-generated name (e.g. "Bot 1"), so that I can tell bots apart in the roster and narration.
4. As the room owner, I want the "Add bot" control disabled once the room is full (4 seats), so that I can't exceed the base-game limit.
5. As the room owner, I want to remove a bot I added while still in the lobby, so that I can free a seat for a human who shows up.
6. As a non-owner player, I want the add/remove-bot controls to be unavailable to me, so that only the owner manages the table composition.
7. As any player in the lobby, I want bots shown in the roster with their name and color and a clear "bot" marker, so that everyone knows which seats are computer-controlled before we start.
8. As the room owner, I want the "Start game" button to become enabled once humans + bots together reach the 3-player minimum, so that I can start a short-handed table.
9. As the room owner, I want to be prevented from adding or removing bots once the game has started, so that the table composition is fixed for the duration of a game.
10. As a player, I want bots to be carried over into a "New game" the same way humans are, so that a re-match keeps the same table without re-adding bots.

### Bots taking turns (general)

11. As a player at a table with bots, I want each bot to take its turn automatically with no human prompting, so that play never waits on a seat nobody controls.
12. As a player, I want a bot's actions to be re-validated by the server just like a human's, so that a bot can never make an illegal move.
13. As a player, I want all of a bot's randomness (dice, stolen card, dev-card draw) generated by the server exactly as for humans, so that bots have no special knowledge or influence over RNG.
14. As a player, I want a bot's hidden information (its resource hand and dev cards) to stay hidden from me, so that bots respect the same secret-hand rules as humans.
15. As a player, I want narration of what a bot did (rolled, built, bought, stole, played a knight, won) just like for humans, so that I can follow the bot's play.
16. As a player, I want a bot turn to resolve promptly, so that watching a bot play doesn't feel like the game has frozen.

### Bots in the setup phase

17. As a player, I want a bot to place its first and second setup settlements on legal vertices respecting the distance rule, so that the snake draft proceeds correctly.
18. As a player, I want a bot to place each setup road on a legal edge adjacent to the settlement it just placed, so that its road network starts connected.
19. As a player, I want a bot's second setup settlement to grant it starting resources per the rules, so that bots enter the main game on equal footing.

### Bots in the main turn loop

20. As a player, I want a bot to roll the dice as the first action of its turn, so that resource production happens on the bot's turn.
21. As a player, I want a bot, after rolling a non-7, to build whatever it can legally afford and chooses to, then end its turn, so that the bot makes forward progress.
22. As a player, I want a bot to prefer higher-value builds (city, then settlement, then road) when it can afford them, so that the bot pursues victory points sensibly.
23. As a player, I want a bot to buy a development card when it has spare resources and a sensible reason to, so that bots use the whole game, not just buildings.
24. As a player, I want a bot to end its turn explicitly when it has nothing useful left to do, so that play passes on without stalling.
25. As a player, I want a bot to never loop forever on its turn, so that the game can't hang on a bot's action phase.

### Bots and the 7 / robber

26. As a player, I want a bot holding more than seven cards on a 7 to discard the required half automatically, so that the robber penalty resolves without waiting.
27. As a player, I want a bot that must move the robber to place it on a tile (preferring one that hurts a leading opponent and not itself) and steal from a legal victim, so that the robber phase completes correctly.
28. As a player, I want a bot to choose a legal robber destination and victim even in awkward board states (e.g. no stealable victim), so that the move never gets stuck.

### Bots and development cards

29. As a player, I want a bot to play at most one development card per turn and never one bought the same turn, so that bots obey the dev-card rules.
30. As a player, I want a bot to play a knight to move the robber and steal (counting toward Largest Army), so that bots can earn the army bonus.
31. As a player, I want a bot to play progress cards (road building, year of plenty, monopoly) with sensible targets, so that bots use their deck.
32. As a player, I want a bot's victory-point dev cards counted toward its total and revealed only on a win, so that bots' hidden VPs work exactly like humans'.

### Bots and trading

33. As the active human player, I want to propose a trade and have each bot respond (accept or decline) promptly, so that my proposal resolves without waiting on a bot.
34. As a player, I want a bot to accept a proposed trade only when it judges the trade good for it (it can pay and wants what's offered), so that bots don't make self-defeating trades.
35. As a player, I want bots to use bank/port trading on their own turns when it unblocks a build they want, so that bots aren't stuck holding unusable surpluses. *(May be deferred — see Out of Scope.)*
36. As a player, I want bots not to initiate player-to-player trade proposals in this version, so that the bot turn stays simple and predictable. *(Out of scope; see below.)*

### Robustness / no-stall guarantees

37. As a player at a mixed table, I want the game to progress through any sequence of bot and human turns to a 10-VP victory, so that a short-handed game always finishes.
38. As a player, I want a fully bot-driven sequence (e.g. several bots in a row) to resolve to completion, so that even an all-bot stretch never freezes the room.
39. As a player, I want the bot driver to be bounded (a guard against infinite loops), so that a logic bug degrades to a skipped/ended action rather than a hung server.

## Implementation Decisions

**Where bot intelligence lives.** A new **pure decision function** in `shared/`,
the highest and primary test seam:

```
decideBotMove(state: GameState, botId: string): BotMove | null
```

- Pure and deterministic given the full `GameState` (the server passes real state;
  the bot is server-side and trusted to read full state — it is *not* limited to a
  projection). Returns the single next intent the bot wants to perform, or `null`
  when it has nothing to do right now (e.g. it is not this bot's turn and it owes
  no discard/trade-response).
- `BotMove` is an **RNG-free intent**, shaped like the existing client→server
  inputs (see `DevCardPlayInput` / `ClientToServerEvents`): the bot says "roll",
  "build road on edge e", "move robber to tile t, steal from victim v", "discard
  these specific cards", "respond accept/decline". It never carries server RNG
  (dice faces, the stolen card, the drawn dev card). The server fills RNG exactly
  as it already does for humans. `BotMove` is a discriminated union mirroring the
  `Action` variants minus RNG fields and minus the actor id (the driver supplies
  `botId`).
- The function is called repeatedly by the driver: each call yields one move; the
  driver applies it and calls again, so a bot's full turn (roll → several builds →
  end turn) emerges as a sequence of single-move decisions rather than a batch.

**Heuristic policy (simple, v1).** The decision function follows a fixed priority
ladder per phase, choosing the first legal/affordable option:
- *Setup settlement:* pick a legal vertex; prefer the highest total pip value
  (production probability) of adjacent tiles as a cheap heuristic, else first legal.
- *Setup road:* any legal edge adjacent to the just-placed settlement.
- *MUST_ROLL:* roll. (Bots do not pre-roll knights in v1.)
- *ACTIONS:* in order — upgrade to a city if affordable and legal; build a
  settlement if affordable and legal; build a road if affordable, legal, and it
  enables a future settlement (or simply if affordable, kept simple); buy a dev
  card if affordable and the deck is non-empty; optionally play one dev card
  (knight to move robber; monopoly/year-of-plenty/road-building when clearly
  useful); otherwise END_TURN. The bot stops building when it can't or chooses not
  to, then ends the turn.
- *DISCARD:* shed the required count, dropping from the most-held resources first
  (deterministic, no RNG needed — the bot reads its own hand from full state).
- *MOVE_ROBBER (and knight target):* choose a tile adjacent to the opponent with
  the most public VP (and not adjacent to the bot itself where avoidable); steal
  from a legal, non-empty victim there, preferring the richest by hand count;
  `null` victim when none.
- *Trade response (bot is a non-active player with an open proposal):* accept only
  if the bot can pay `want` and the deal is non-losing by a simple count
  heuristic; otherwise decline.

The ladder is intentionally rigid and legality-checked against the same helpers
the reducer/affordances use, so a bot move handed back to `reduce` always passes.

**Reducer / type changes (`shared/`).**
- `Player` gains `isBot: boolean` (default `false`; humans set it false on JOIN).
- New action `ADD_BOT { actorId, playerId, name, color }`: owner-only, `LOBBY`
  only, validated like `JOIN` (color available, room not full, name non-empty);
  seats a `Player` with `isBot: true`, `connected: true`, `vacant: false`, empty
  hand. Emits a `PLAYER_JOINED`-style event (or a dedicated `BOT_ADDED`) so the
  roster/narration update.
- New action `REMOVE_BOT { actorId, playerId }`: owner-only, `LOBBY` only, target
  must be a bot; removes that seat. Emits a removal event.
- The owner is still `players[0]`. Bots are appended like any seat; a bot can never
  be `players[0]` because a human owner must exist first to add it.
- `START_GAME`, `NEW_GAME`, projection, setup order, and turn machine are
  otherwise unchanged — a bot is just a `Player` with `isBot: true`, so all
  existing rules apply to it unmodified. `NEW_GAME` already keeps all seats, so
  bots carry over for free.
- Projection: `PlayerView` gains `isBot: boolean` so clients can render the marker.

**Server bot driver (`server/`).** A new driver that mirrors the existing
`settleVacantSeats()` auto-driver in `room.ts`:
- After every applied action (in `Room.apply`, alongside / folded into the vacant-
  seat settling pass), run a bounded loop: while the seat that currently owes input
  is a **bot**, ask `decideBotMove`, translate the returned `BotMove` into a full
  `Action` (filling RNG with the room's existing sources — `rollDice` for ROLL,
  `pickStolenCard` for robber/knight, the server dev-deck for buys), `dispatch` it,
  and repeat. A guard bound (similar to the existing `players.length * N + k`)
  prevents infinite loops; if exceeded, the driver stops (defensive).
- "Owes input" cases the driver must cover: it is a bot's turn (SETUP placement,
  MUST_ROLL, ACTIONS, MOVE_ROBBER); a bot owes a discard during `DISCARD`; a bot
  must respond to an open human trade proposal during `ACTIONS`.
- Bots have **no socket and no token** — they exist only as seats in `GameState`.
  `handleDisconnect`, vacancy timers, and reclaim logic never apply to bots (a bot
  seat is always "connected" and never goes vacant). The driver, not a socket
  handler, is the bot's input source.
- The RNG-filling step reuses the exact translation the socket handlers already do
  (e.g. the `moveRobber`/`playDevCard` knight handlers that call
  `pickStolenCard`), so bot and human actions are indistinguishable to `reduce`.

**Client (`client/`).** Lobby gains owner-only "Add bot" / "Remove bot" controls
(disabled when full / not owner). The roster renders a bot marker from
`PlayerView.isBot`. No in-game client changes are required: bots act server-side
and their effects arrive through the normal snapshot + event stream, so existing
screens render bot turns with no special handling.

**Protocol (`shared/`).** Two new client→server events, `addBot` and `removeBot`
(owner-gated server-side), wired in `app.ts` like the other intents. No new
server→client message types are needed — bot moves surface through existing
`snapshot` / `events`.

## Testing Decisions

Good tests here assert **external behavior, not implementation details**, matching
the established reducer test style: given an input `GameState`, assert the decision
function's chosen `BotMove`, or the post-`reduce` state/events — never internal
helpers. Because the bot is a pure function over full state, its tests need no
server, socket, RNG mocking, or browser.

Seams to test (highest first):

1. **The bot decision function in `shared/` (primary seam).** `decideBotMove(state,
   botId)` carries the bulk of v2 behavior and gets the bulk of the tests:
   - Setup: returns a legal distance-rule-respecting settlement, then a legal
     adjacent road; covers both snake-draft rounds.
   - MUST_ROLL: returns a roll move.
   - ACTIONS: returns the affordable high-priority build (city > settlement >
     road); returns a dev-card buy when flush; returns END_TURN when nothing useful
     remains; never returns an unaffordable or illegal move.
   - DISCARD: returns a discard of exactly the required count, drawn from the
     largest holdings, summing correctly.
   - MOVE_ROBBER: returns a legal tile (≠ current robber tile) and a legal,
     non-empty victim (or null when none), preferring a leading opponent.
   - Dev cards: respects one-per-turn and not-bought-this-turn; knight returns a
     robber target + victim.
   - Trade response: returns accept only when payable + non-losing, else decline.
   - Determinism: the same state yields the same move (no hidden RNG).
   - Termination: from any in-game state where it's the bot's turn, a finite
     apply-loop of `decideBotMove` reaches END_TURN (no infinite ladder).

2. **The new reducer actions in `shared/` (existing seam).** `ADD_BOT` /
   `REMOVE_BOT`: owner-gating, LOBBY-only, color-clash and room-full rejection,
   `isBot` set, removal targets only bots, roster/event correctness. Mirror the
   existing lobby (`join` / `startGame`) tests.

3. **The server bot driver (`server/`, lighter integration).** Drive the server
   (reusing the `seats.test.ts` harness) and assert table-level behavior that only
   exists at the connection/driver level: a mixed human+bot game progresses without
   waiting on bot seats; a stretch of consecutive bot turns resolves; a bot
   discards on a human-rolled 7; a bot responds to a human's trade proposal; and a
   sufficiently-played game can end in a bot victory. Kept thin — rule and policy
   correctness live in seam 1, not here. Assert the driver's loop guard terminates.

Prior art: `shared/test/*.test.ts` (reducer/policy style) for seams 1–2;
`server/test/seats.test.ts` (server harness, auto-driver behavior) for seam 3. The
bot driver is the direct analogue of `settleVacantSeats`, already exercised there.

## Out of Scope

- **Strong / strategic AI.** v2 ships one simple heuristic level only. No board
  evaluation beyond pip-count at setup, no lookahead, no opponent modeling, no
  difficulty levels (the earlier "easy/normal" option is explicitly deferred).
- **Bot-initiated player-to-player trades.** Bots only *respond* to human trade
  proposals; they never propose trades to humans or to each other in v2.
- **Bots taking over disconnected / vacant human seats.** v2 only adds bots
  explicitly in the lobby. The existing vacant-seat auto-skip behavior is
  unchanged; converting a dropped human into a bot mid-game is deferred.
- **Owner replacing a human with a bot mid-game.** Deferred.
- **Bot personalities, names chosen by the owner, or bot avatars** beyond an
  auto-assigned "Bot N" name and the standard color marker.
- **Tunable bot speed / artificial "thinking" delay.** v2 bots act as fast as the
  server can drive them; pacing/animation polish is out of scope.
- Everything already out of scope in v1 (expansions, accounts/DB, multiple rooms,
  chat, persistence across restarts, horizontal scaling).

## Further Notes

- The design leans entirely on v1's load-bearing decisions: the pure reducer with
  injected RNG means the bot can be a pure function whose moves are re-validated by
  the same `reduce`, and the server-side `settleVacantSeats` auto-driver is the
  exact pattern the bot driver copies. A bot is "just a `Player` with `isBot:
  true`" precisely because the rules engine never special-cased who an actor is.
- Highest-risk area: the bot driver's termination. The action phase is a loop of
  bot decisions, so the decision ladder **must** always reach END_TURN, and the
  driver must carry a hard guard. Both are called out as explicit tests.
- The decision function reading *full* `GameState` (not a projection) is a
  deliberate, safe exception to the anti-cheat boundary: bots run inside the
  trusted server and never emit their reasoning to clients — only their resulting,
  re-validated actions, whose effects are projected normally. Bots gain no
  information advantage that surfaces to any client.
- Suggested build order: `isBot` + `ADD_BOT`/`REMOVE_BOT` reducer actions and lobby
  UI (visible, testable end-to-end at the table) → `decideBotMove` setup + roll +
  end-turn (a bot that plays a trivial valid turn) → build/buy/dev policy → robber
  + discard + trade-response → server driver wiring + termination guard → polish.
